Current project · Ongoing

Retail Consumption and Satellite Nighttime Lights: Measuring Local Economic Change

With Prof. Matias Carrasco Kind and Brandon Buncher · Research Fellow, Data Science Research Services, UIUCNielsenIQ · NASA VIIRS

Satellite nighttime lights are widely used to study economic activity, but increases in illumination alone may not reveal whether observed changes reflect sustained economic development or temporary sources of light. This project asks whether retail consumption patterns can strengthen the interpretation of nighttime-light changes as indicators of persistent local economic activity.

My work focuses on constructing comparable retail consumption measures across time, including patterns in staples, essential goods, and categories potentially linked to household settlement. I built a harmonized processing workflow for two generations of NielsenIQ scanner data and assembled a balanced panel covering 205 U.S. Designated Market Areas for a comparison of 2018 and 2023.

Research status: The retail-scanner component and exploratory analysis are underway. The comparison with VIIRS nighttime-light measures is a planned next stage; we do not yet claim a demonstrated relationship or causal contribution.

Next steps include integrating satellite measures, examining settlement-sensitive consumption categories, comparing urban and suburban patterns, and validating the retail results through robustness checks and outside datasets.

Project methods: Python, Dask, data harmonization, geospatial aggregation, causal inference, statistical methods, machine learning. Research figures will be added when suitable for public sharing.
